# Plain Sonnet 5.5 against the app, on the same golden data (#781)

## Why

The app claims its chat pipeline keeps the model from stating wrong figures. No eval had tested that claim against
the model on its own. The bare arm sends the eval's questions to the same model with the whole frozen golden dataset
in its system prompt, and no pipeline. Both arms answer from the same data, so the difference is the pipeline.

## Method

| arm | what answers | runs (2026-11-25, UTC) |
|---|---|---|
| app | analyst-mode stack at 060549c1, `CHAT_ANALYST_AGENT=1`, answers by `anthropic/claude-sonnet-5-5`, judge `anthropic/claude-haiku-5-5` | 014331, 014811, 015316 |
| bare | `scripts/research-quality-bare.ts` at 060549c1, `anthropic/claude-sonnet-5-5` at API defaults (adaptive thinking) | 013902, 014547, 015253 |

- Dataset 9714d6e9 and question set 2a1d31d1: 12 questions, 14 turns, in both arms.
- **Figure check.** `scripts/research-quality-figures.ts` ran on every run. It also ran on a fourth bare run, 011555, made at
  8b5aae41 on the same dataset.
- **Blind scoring.** Pairings 020210, 020217 and 020219 pair app run i with bare run i. One Gemini 4 Neon reviewer scored
  all 36 pairs from the packets and `company-data.md`, the bare arm's data brief. It never saw the keys or the run files.
- **Not blind in practice.** The reviewer could tell the arms apart by style. It called them "engine-cited" and
  "narrative" answers. It did not know which arm was the product.
- **Cut short.** The Anthropic key reached its spend limit at about 02:00 UTC. Two more app runs failed partway and are
  left out. The planned control runs never started: bare at `--effort low`, bare on Haiku 5.5, and bare on Opus 5.5.

## Figures

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| app | 3 | 473 | 11.3 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| bare | 4 | 2,174 | 38.8 | 25 | 16 | 9 | 163 |

I read every "other wrong" verdict against the data.

- **One is a real misstatement, counted twice.** The model gave AMD's Q4 FY2025 operating margin as 11.7%, where the
  data is 14.08%. It retracted the figure in the next turn, and the checker also counts the retraction, which quotes it.
- **One is an approximation read strictly.** "About 10 to 15 basis points per quarter" describes AMD's last two
  operating-margin gains, which are 13 and 17 basis points.
- **Six are labeller errors, and the model was right.**
  - "Gross margin fell to 60.5%" was signed negative.
  - The 2026-08-28 closes, $178.34 and $162.27, were pointed at 2026-08-31.

The blind reviewer found one misstatement that the check missed. A bare answer said AMD's gross margin was "54.0% for
four straight quarters", but the first of those quarters is 53.6%.

A random sample of 14 of the 163 not-checkable figures, checked by hand, holds no error. These figures are
trailing-four-quarter sums, "adding the $800M back" adjustments, and gaps between companies.

The bare arm made 2 real misstatements in 2,174 figures, or 0.09%. With the 16 last-digit slips the rate is 18 in
2,174, or 0.83%.

## Against the decision rule in #781

- **Accuracy.** The rule asks for a bare wrong-figure rate within about one point of the app's. The bare arm's rate is
  0.83% with slips and 0.09% without, against the app's 0%. The rule holds.
- **Quality.** The rule asks for higher conclusions, counterarguments and decision-useful scores, by more than the spread
  between runs. The deltas are +26, +24 and +40 over 30 pairs, and every pairing moves the same way by 6 to 14 points.
  The rule holds.

On this question set and model, the output pipeline costs more than it saves. Four things limit that reading:

1. **Effort.** The bare arm thought at the API's default effort. The app's answer model asks for low effort. Some of the
   quality gap may come from thinking effort, not from the pipeline. The `--effort low` control answers this, and it has
   not run.
2. **Retrieval.** The dataset is three companies in 42 KB of text, so the bare arm had every fact in front of it. The run
   tests the output pipeline, not retrieval. At the app's scale, a model needs tools to find its facts.
3. **Size of the sample.** There were three pairings, one reviewer and one model. The deltas are large and consistent,
   but the model sweep (Haiku, Opus) has not run.
4. **Provenance.** The bare arm's figures were right, but a reader could not click through to their source. The app's
   links have real value, and this rubric prices them only in `figures_match_sources`.
