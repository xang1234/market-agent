# Plain Claude against the app, on the same golden data (#781)

## Why

The app claims its chat pipeline keeps the model from stating wrong figures. No eval had tested that claim against the
model on its own. The bare arm sends the eval's questions to a model with the whole frozen golden dataset in its system
prompt, and no pipeline. Both arms answer from the same data, so the difference between them is the pipeline.

- `no_invented_numbers` gives 1 for an unstated gap. That is where the app loses its points: on the AMD segments and the

Every reviewer named the same app failures, in every pairing. None of them is a wrong number:

- Every app run dropped one company from the three-company comparison: AMD in app runs 1 to 3, NVDA in run 4 and AAPL
  in run 5.
- It covered only part of AMD's eight-quarter margin trend.
- It did not show NVIDIA's segment table.
- It missed AMD's filed explanation in `cause-in-evidence`.
- It left the missing AMD segments unstated.

The read and model-call limits explain the first three. On main, the three-company question hit the model-call limit
in 5 of 5 runs (2026-11-25-standout-findings-nvda-10y.md).

## Against the decision rule in #781

- **Accuracy.** The rule asks for a bare wrong-figure rate within about one point of the app's.
  - Sonnet holds it, at both efforts, and so does Opus. Their real misstatements are 0.08% to 0.21% of checked figures,
    and they score above the app on `no_invented_numbers`.
  - Haiku holds it on the figure check, at 0.60%. It falls below the app on `no_invented_numbers` (59 against 66), so on
    the rubric it does not hold.
- **Quality.** The rule asks for higher conclusions, counterarguments and decision-useful scores, by more than the spread
  between runs. Every arm holds it. The smallest gap is Haiku's +21 on counterarguments over 30 pairs.
- **Effort.** At the app's own low effort, Sonnet scores 60/60 on conclusions against the app's 27/60. Thinking effort
  does not explain the gap.

At Sonnet strength and above, the output pipeline costs more than it saves. At Haiku it still prevents real errors.
The value of the wording guards falls as the model gets stronger.

## What this run does not test

1. **Retrieval.** The dataset is three companies in 42 KB of text, so each bare arm had every fact in front of it. At
   the app's scale, a model needs tools to find its facts, and finding the right fact is the data layer's job.
2. **Provenance.** Bare figures were nearly all right, but a reader could not click through to their source. The app's
   links have real value, and this rubric prices them only in `figures_match_sources`.
3. **Reasoning slips.** The remaining risk in free prose is a claim that relates right figures wrongly. Neither the
   figure check nor the rubric measures that rate.
4. **Breadth.** All the answers come from one model family and all the scores from one reviewer family.

## Limits of the figure check

- One numeral gets one source. A figure claimed for several periods, as in "54.0% for four straight quarters", passes
  when one period matches. #781 tracks the fix.
- The app's charts render as titles without their series, so their values are not counted. They are drawn from stored
  prices by code. The bare arms' returns in tables are counted.
- A word sign applies only to amounts and changes. A margin, share or ratio keeps only the sign in its text.
- A round number's trailing zeros are not significant, so "60%" passes for any value from 55% to 65%.

The run files and `<run>.figures.json` stay under `.dev/eval-runs/research-quality/` in the checkout that made them.
Each figures file is 216 KB to 891 KB, so none is committed. `rejudge` rescores one from its saved labels.
