Offline and dev only (#610). Inputs are the engine's selection as of the 10-K filing date + 1 day, with each SEC fact taken as public by the end of its filing date in New York. The price is the first close on or after that day, a yfinance split-adjusted close, never certified. Required growth is the growth of total free cash flow that price requires at its multiple, with ADR 0006's model, the 10% required return and d, the share change stock-based compensation implies. Trailing growth is the same total free cash flow's growth to this 10-K from the ones 3 and 5 fiscal years earlier, each read as of its own filing date + 1 day, so known at the as-of date. Realized growth is its growth to the 10-K five fiscal years later; the realized share change over those years, buybacks included, sits beside d. Each growth is also shown per share, the earlier count restated for splits. The price return is annualized over five years from that close, price only, with no dividends.

## Answer

**No. In this sample the gap does not sort 5-year price returns.** #730 asks whether trailing growth minus required growth, both known at the 10-K filing date, sorts what followed. Neither split above shows an order.

- **3-year gap.** 40 rows from 11 issuers. Rows at or above 0 had a median 9.15% a year (24 rows, 8 at or above the 10% hurdle). Rows below 0 had 8.61% (16 rows, 5 at or above). By third, lowest gap to highest, the medians are 8.61% (14 rows), 6.13% (13) and 9.78% (13). The middle third is the worst, so there is no rank order.
- **5-year gap.** 39 rows from 12 issuers. At or above 0, 9.02% (20 rows, 7 at or above the hurdle). Below 0, 8.91% (19 rows, 6 at or above). By third, 9.32% (13 rows), 8.87% (13) and 9.08% (13). The thirds are flat.
- **Against hindsight.** Split by realized growth, the next five years' actual growth, the same names show 12.72% at or above required against 6.70% below. That spread does not survive the move to what was known at the filing date. A trailing record of beating required growth did not mark the rows that went on to beat it.

**What this sample can and cannot show.** About 15 surviving large caps with overlapping 5-year windows can show which way the gap leans. They cannot establish that it sorts returns, or that it does not.

- The buckets hold 13 or 14 rows from 5 to 8 issuers each. Six issuers (ABT, AMGN, JNJ, KO, MCD and TMO) give up to 5 priced rows each to a gap split, so a few names carry each bucket.
- One issuer's rows in consecutive years share 4 of their 5 years of returns, so the rows are far from independent and the effective N is well below the row count.
- Every issuer is a large company that exists today. A gap screen is meant to flag names that fall short, and the names that fell furthest short are the ones this sample cannot contain.

So the sample leans nowhere. It gives no evidence for the universe screen on the gap (#736). A test that could settle the question needs a broad point-in-time universe that includes delisted names.

**Buybacks.** Over the 61 rows with both realized figures and a d, free cash flow per share grew a median 5.47% a year against 4.03% in total. The share count fell a median 1.11% a year, where d, the share change stock-based compensation implies, is 0.22%. Required growth leaves buybacks out (ADR 0006), so realized growth stays total, and the per-share figure sits beside it.

## How it was run

The runbook is the header of `services/fundamentals/src/fcf-backtest.ts`. Everything in this doc except the Answer, How it was run and Caveats sections is the script's output. Those three come from this notes file through `--notes`. From `services/fundamentals`, the command above writes it with `> ../../docs/eval-runs/fcf-backtest/2026-11-21.md`.

- The stack was this checkout's own Compose project at port offset 395. Only its Postgres ran (port 54724), migrated and seeded. No app service ran.
- The weekly reference import loaded 10,054 listings from the GitHub release bundle dated 2026-10-05.
- **SEC requests.** Every SEC call went through a fetch guard preloaded with `--import`, the 2026-11-11 run's guard with two changes. It also caches `www.sec.gov/files/company_tickers.json`, and it keeps one request log for every process, capped at 30. The run made 26 requests. One was `company_tickers.json` for `fill:ciks`, which filled 5,936 issuers. The other 25 were companyfacts downloads for the 25 issuers, all HTTP 200. The guard refused the submissions endpoint before any request, so the backfill logs its acceptance-metadata fetch as failed and no fact carries an acceptance time. The bodies are cached under `.dev/sec-cache` in the checkout that ran this, so a rerun makes no request.
- `backfill:sec-statements --annual-history-years 16` completed for 24 issuers. Costco's stopped on a `metric-mapper` unit error (`eps_basic` in unit `pure`) after storing 15 of its 16 fiscal years. The missing one is FY2019, which COST tagged `fp` Q4, so it reads `no_fy_facts` as it did on 2026-11-11. JNJ's FY2024 balance sheet failed extraction again (`CommercialPaper` period disagreement). FY2024 is FY+5 of JNJ FY2019, whose realized growth reads only free cash flow and the share count, so that row still has one.
- Splits came from Polygon `sync:splits` for the 25 listings. 20 hit HTTP 429 and were retried one at a time.
- Closes came from yfinance through the dev-providers sidecar (`capture:closes` on 2026-11-21), ten years from 2016-11-21 to 2026-11-20. ABT and AMGN timed out at first and were retried.
- The backtest itself ran with the guard at a fetch cap of 0 and no SEC user agent set, and it counted 0 SEC requests.

Two rows differ from 2026-11-11 beyond the new columns. NKE FY2016 now reads its own 10-K, which the 16-year history holds, so it is no longer a `period_mismatch`. It has no cover count, so its shares read `stale_input`, and its as-of day precedes the closes. SBUX filed its FY2026 10-K after 2026-11-11, so SBUX FY2021 now has realized growth. MCD FY2021's `d` also moves from 0.07% to 0.08% (0.00075046), because the backtest now keeps a ratio's full precision before rounding (4665362b).

## Caveats

- **Publication rule.** A fact counts as public by the end of its filing's SEC filing date in New York. The backtest applies this bound in memory, beside each fact's own proofs. It is never stored and never certifies anything (`services/fundamentals/src/fcf-backtest.ts`, header).
- **Trailing growth is as first filed.** Each 10-K 3 or 5 fiscal years earlier is read as of its own filing date + 1 day, so a later restatement of that year is not used, even when it was public by the row's as-of date. FY+5 is read the same way. Both are known before the row's as-of day.
- **Who reaches a gap.** 69 rows have a required growth outside a `period_mismatch`. 21 lack a 3-year trailing figure and 24 a 5-year one, nearly all `blocked_by_dependency`, because the earlier 10-K lacks the certified operating cash flow or capex concept. SBUX's negative FY2014 free cash flow gives its FY2017 3-year and FY2019 5-year rows `non_positive_base`. COST, HON, TXN and UNP have no row in one gap or the other. Then 8 3-year and 6 5-year rows have no 5-year price.
- **Prices.** Closes are yfinance split-adjusted `daily_close_captures` on the dev DB, never certified. Each row is priced at the first close on or after its as-of day, at most 4 days later. 45 of the 137 priced rows are priced after their as-of day, 38 by 2 days and 7 by 3. The captures start 2016-11-21, so 8 FY2016 rows read `stale_price`: COST, MSFT, PG, WMT, NKE, CSCO, ORCL and QCOM.
- **Late FY2021 rows have no 5-year price.** The latest captured close is 2026-11-20, so a row has a 5-year price only when its first close is on or before 2021-11-20. 9 rows with a required growth have none, all FY2021. SBUX FY2021, filed 2021-11-19 and first priced 2021-11-22, is one.
- **The hurdle is price only.** The 5-year return is the change in the adjusted close, with no dividends, so high-yield names show less than their holders earned.
- **Ties.** Required growth is the solver's bisection midpoint, within 0.005 points. No gap in either split is that close to zero. The nearest are MCD FY2019 at -0.065 points (5-year) and MCD FY2020 at 0.251 (3-year).
- **Outliers stay in.** SBUX FY2020 shows a 1,084.72x multiple on depressed FY2020 free cash flow, a 3-year gap of -75.11 points and a 5-year gap of -55.96. SBUX FY2018 has the largest gaps, 60.99 and 42.76 points. Medians and thirds by rank limit their pull, but each still sits in a third of 13 or 14 rows.
- **Small N and survivorship.** 61 rows with both realized and required growth come from 15 of the 25 issuers (CAT, PEP, WMT, HD, NKE, INTC, ORCL, MMM, QCOM and MRK give none), and every issuer is a large company that exists today.
- **AAPL and GOOGL are skipped.** #597 keeps their listings' issuers apart from the SEC issuers.
- **Filer tags mislabel rows.** HON tagged its FY2021 10-K as fiscal 2020, so its FY2020 row is a `period_mismatch` and FY2021 is `no_fy_facts`. NKE reports no `dei:EntityCommonStockSharesOutstanding`, so its FY2016 to FY2018 shares read `stale_input` and FY2019 to FY2021 are `no_filing_date`. COST tagged its FY2019 10-K as `fp` Q4.
