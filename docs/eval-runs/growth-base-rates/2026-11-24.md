# Growth base rates, 2026-11-24

How often did companies of a similar size deliver the growth a Call's price requires? This run builds that base rate from SEC's bulk company facts and sets three Calls against it. A base rate is a frequency of reported history, not a probability.

## How it was built

- **Archive.** One download of `https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/daily-index/xbrl/companyfacts.zip` (the URL on SEC's EDGAR API page) on 2026-11-24, with the fair-access user agent. 1,429,204,054 bytes, sha256 `72bc1c52670fe57a3d701f13cbab55763c122388b314d1eaca921bb18ddfbacd`, cached at `~/.local/share/market-agent/sec-bulk/companyfacts-2026-11-24.zip`. No other SEC request was made.
- **Command.** `npm run --silent build:growth-base-rates -- --archive ~/.local/share/market-agent/sec-bulk/companyfacts-2026-11-24.zip` in `services/fundamentals` wrote `services/financial-core/data/growth-base-rates.json` (600 KB). A second build from the cached windows (`--windows`) wrote a byte-identical file.
- **Windows.** One filer's fiscal year t to t + 5, for every t up to FY2020, so every end year is FY2025 or earlier and was due by spring 2026. Each figure is the one its own 10-K reports, and periods are dated by their dates, never the `fy` tag.
- **Measured quantities.** Free cash flow per share, the quantity the Call solves for. Free cash flow is operating cash flow less capex in `free_cash_flow.v1`'s concepts. Per share uses diluted weighted-average shares, carried across splits through each next 10-K's restated comparative. Total free cash flow and revenue are measured beside it.
- **Buckets.** By starting revenue, nominal USD.

## Coverage

- 20,613 filers read, start years FY2003 to FY2020. Starts before FY2009 come only from comparatives in the first XBRL 10-Ks.
- 58,085 windows have a starting free cash flow. 14,858 of them (26%) have no starting revenue in the engine's three revenue concepts, so no bucket.
- Of the 43,227 bucketed windows, 13,272 have a free cash flow per share growth. The rest are named outcomes:

| Outcome | Windows |
|---|---|
| non_positive_base (free cash flow at or below zero at the start) | 16,445 |
| ended_early (the filer's last 10-K is before year 5) | 6,432 |
| incompatible_definition (the two ends use different capex or cash flow concepts) | 3,458 |
| missing_shares (a split-chain link or a count is missing) | 2,735 |
| missing_end (still filing, but no year-5 figure) | 885 |

An ended-early window is counted beside N, never in it. A filer stops filing when it is acquired as well as when it fails, so N can lean toward survivors (inferred, not measured). A window that ended at or below zero is in N at −100%.

The smallest `fcf_per_share` bucket holds 377 windows, above the 100 the Call needs.

**Under USD 100M of revenue, the bear growth is the −100% floor.** p25 of free cash flow per share is −100% there, because more than a quarter of those windows (434 of 1,367 measured, before the 10,017 that started at or below zero) ended at or below zero. ADR 0006 needs growth above −100%, so V at −100% has no value in the model (`growth_out_of_range`). A Call in that bucket declines under rule 0, an input range outcome, not rule 2. Rule 2 needs a computed B at or below zero. Taken as a limit, B at −100% is net cash per share, which is positive for a filer with net cash.

## Caveats

- **Per share is not `g`.** ADR 0006's `g` is free cash flow growth before dilution, and buybacks are left out. Historical growth per share includes buybacks. In the USD 50B and up bucket the median is 6.7% per share against 4.2% in total. The fcf-backtest run of 2026-11-21 shows the same gap: 5.47% against 4.03%. The owner chose per share. The table also holds total free cash flow.
- **Nominal buckets.** Revenue is not deflated, so an FY2005 start of USD 10B sits beside an FY2020 start of USD 10B.
- **10-K only.** Amendments (10-K/A) are not read, and a filer that tags revenue in other concepts has no bucket.
