# Growth base rates, 2026-11-24

How often did companies of a similar size deliver the growth a Call's price requires? This run builds that base rate from SEC's bulk company facts and sets three Calls against it. A base rate is a frequency of reported history, not a probability.

Updated on 2026-11-25 for the owner's base case (ADR 0005, "Base case"). Both cases and the base rate now read total free cash flow growth (`fcf`), and a Call states a stance.

Rebuilt on 2026-11-25 from the same archive, after PR #776's review (no SEC request). Three builder fixes moved counts: operating cash flow and capex covering different periods in one year are now `mismatched_period` instead of being subtracted; a filer whose year-5 10-K lacks the tracked figures is now `missing_end` instead of `ended_early`; and the windows cache is keyed by the archive's sha256. The Call now counts the base rate at the required growth rounded to the table's 4 stored places and prints that cutoff (7.33% for AAPL, not 7.3%), and prints each case's growth at that precision. Every figure below is the rebuilt table's. The AAPL probe still falls short at the same H.

## How it was built

- **Archive.** One download of `https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/daily-index/xbrl/companyfacts.zip` (the URL on SEC's EDGAR API page) on 2026-11-24, with the fair-access user agent. 1,429,204,054 bytes, sha256 `72bc1c52670fe57a3d701f13cbab55763c122388b314d1eaca921bb18ddfbacd`, cached at `~/.local/share/market-agent/sec-bulk/companyfacts-2026-11-24.zip`. No other SEC request was made.
- **Command.** `npm run --silent build:growth-base-rates -- --archive ~/.local/share/market-agent/sec-bulk/companyfacts-2026-11-24.zip` in `services/fundamentals` wrote `services/financial-core/data/growth-base-rates/547384e18543e4178e40539c719059f5e5d55965381205a48a7f43b459e41abd.json` (600 KB), named by its own sha256, and pointed `CURRENT` at it. A second build from the cached windows (`--windows`) wrote a byte-identical file. A saved Call names the table it read (`base_rate_table`), and the seal regenerates it from that file.
- **Windows.** One filer's fiscal year t to t + 5, for every t up to FY2020, so every end year is FY2025 or earlier and was due by spring 2026. Each figure is the one its own 10-K reports, and periods are dated by their dates, never the `fy` tag.
- **Measured quantities.** Free cash flow per share, the quantity the Call solves for. Free cash flow is operating cash flow less capex in `free_cash_flow.v1`'s concepts. Per share uses diluted weighted-average shares, carried across splits through each next 10-K's restated comparative. Total free cash flow and revenue are measured beside it.
- **Buckets.** By starting revenue, nominal USD.

## Coverage

- 20,613 filers read, start years FY2003 to FY2020. Starts before FY2009 come only from comparatives in the first XBRL 10-Ks.
- 58,085 windows have a starting free cash flow. 14,858 of them (26%) have no starting revenue in the engine's three revenue concepts, so no bucket.
- Of the 43,227 bucketed windows, 13,265 have a free cash flow per share growth. The rest are named outcomes:

| Outcome | Windows |
|---|---|
| non_positive_base (free cash flow at or below zero at the start) | 16,426 |
| ended_early (the filer's last 10-K is before year 5) | 6,384 |
| incompatible_definition (the two ends use different capex or cash flow concepts) | 3,455 |
| missing_shares (a split-chain link or a count is missing) | 2,732 |
| missing_end (a 10-K for year 5 or later, but no year-5 figure) | 933 |
| mismatched_period (cash flow and capex at one end cover different periods) | 32 |

Whether a filer reached year 5 is read from its 10-Ks, any us-gaap fact: each 10-K is for the year its latest annual period ends in, or its latest balance when it states no annual period.

An ended-early window is counted beside N, never in it. A filer stops filing when it is acquired as well as when it fails, so N can lean toward survivors (inferred, not measured). A window that ended at or below zero is in N at −100%.

The smallest `fcf` bucket holds 430 windows, above the 100 the Call needs. Each median is below the default 10% required return. The 50B-and-up bucket's p25 is rank 108 of 430 and its median rank 215. Its one `mismatched_period` window left N, and the point at rank 108 moved from −5.17% to −4.93%.

**Under USD 100M of revenue, the bear growth is the −100% floor.** p25 of total free cash flow is −100% there, because more than a quarter of those windows (777 of 2,050 measured, before the 10,003 that started at or below zero) ended at or below zero. ADR 0006 needs growth above −100%, so B at −100% has no value in the model (`growth_out_of_range`). A Call in that bucket declines under rule 0, an input range outcome, not rule 2. Rule 2 needs a computed B at or below zero. Taken as a limit, B at −100% is net cash per share, which is positive for a filer with net cash. The bucket's median, −10.1%, has a value.

## Would most large-cap Calls fall short at the default settings?

Yes. The driver is the anchored exit multiple, together with a median growth below the required return. The bear growth and the bear-loss limit are not the driver.

A synthetic large cap measured with `callStance`: F0 = 1 per share, no net cash, d = 0.277% (AAPL's), the default settings, and an anchor of the lower of today's multiple and the 17.33x cap. That assumes its own ten-year median EV/FCF is above today's multiple, as AAPL's is.

| Bucket | Clears | Declined, rule 3 | Falls short | Binding test at 17.33x |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| USD 50B and up | below 7.0x P/FCF | 7.0x to 13.0x | 13.0x and up | value, H = 13.84x |
| USD 10B to 50B | below 6.3x | 6.3x to 11.3x | 11.3x and up | value, H = 13.13x |
| USD 1B to 10B | below 6.0x | 6.0x to 9.3x | 9.3x and up | bear-loss, H = 11.66x |

At the default 10% near band no price states "Near your hurdle" in these cases: a one-point move shifts H by about 4%, so the band is never stable. AAPL trades at 36.6x its FY2026 free cash flow. Most large caps trade above 13x (inferred, not measured here).

| Input moved | H as a multiple of FCF per share |
|---|---|
| None (X = 17.33x) | 13.84x |
| X = 25.85x (AAPL's ten-year median, no cap) | 18.97x |
| X = 36.15x (AAPL's today's multiple) | 25.18x |
| Required return 8%, so the cap is 26x | 20.76x |
| Margin of safety 1 | 15.35x (the bear-loss test then binds; 15.19x before the rebuild) |
| Bear-loss limit 50% | 13.84x (no change) |

- **The exit multiple.** At a 10% required return the cap holds X at or below 17.33x, and X is never above today's multiple. Below the cap a cheaper price lowers X, and H falls with it. AAPL falls short from USD 125 up, declines under rule 3 from USD 68 to USD 124, and clears at USD 67 and below, where today's multiple is 6.74x (whole-dollar prices).
- **The median growth.** Every bucket's median, 3.0% to 4.4% for the buckets above USD 100M, is below 10%. Discounted, the five years of cash flow shrink, and V stays under 1.25 times a price set at the same multiple.
- **The bear case and the bear-loss limit.** In both large-cap buckets the value test binds before the bear-loss test. Doubling the limit leaves H unchanged.

Whether to change a setting or an anchor is the owner's decision. Nothing here changes one.

## Caveats

- **Per share is not `g`.** ADR 0006's `g` is free cash flow growth before dilution, and buybacks are left out. Historical growth per share includes buybacks, so as `g` it would count them twice. In the USD 50B and up bucket the median is 6.7% per share against 4.2% in total. The fcf-backtest run of 2026-11-21 shows the same gap: 5.47% against 4.03%. The owner chose per share on 2026-11-24 and total free cash flow on 2026-11-25.
- **Required-return moves keep X.** The stability rule's required-return move leaves the anchored exit multiple where it was, though the 4% cap depends on the required return. Moving both would widen the move.
- **Nominal buckets.** Revenue is not deflated, so an FY2005 start of USD 10B sits beside an FY2020 start of USD 10B.
- **10-K only.** Amendments (10-K/A) are not read, and a filer that tags revenue in other concepts has no bucket.
